Controls

Sidestep caps how far one evasion manoeuvre may travel. At the maximum it is unbounded, which is the long-standing behaviour; below it, the manoeuvre stops at that distance and the robot resumes its route.
The 30 px default is set deliberately and is not an improvement: in the sweep recorded in warehouse_sim.js a 30 px budget measured 42% of baseline deliveries, and no budget on that sweep reduced sidestep travel at all. Shorter is worse because a robot that stops short of clearing an obstacle is re-blocked next tick and sidesteps again.
Expect robots to freeze at this setting, and it is not a bug in the page. 30 px is the dominant cause of permanent two-robot deadlock: over 15 seeds and 960 s each it produces nine stalls of eight seconds or more, the worst lasting 887 s — i.e. the rest of the run — against one at the unbounded default. Two robots meet, neither manoeuvre is long enough to clear the other, and they alternate re-blocks instead of resolving. Drag this to the maximum to watch them recover; the low end is here to make the failure visible, and this is what it looks like when it runs for a whole session.
Below 21 px (two robot radii plus the safety margin) a robot cannot clear another robot at all, so the low end does not shorten the manoeuvre — it makes each one fail and start again, and you will see the swerving get worse rather than better.

Lidar range is how far the cone reaches and lidar angle is its half-width, defaulting to 70 px and 20°. Together they set how early a robot notices a crossing counterpart, and the angle is the one that matters here: it is the difference between a robot that yields and one that simply stops.
Set the angle below about 18° and the demo deadlocks, visibly and permanently. The hard collision check refuses to let two robots overlap, so a robot is stopped at 21 px — but if the other robot is off to the side, outside the cone, nothing is ever reported: no obstacle, no question to the model, no action, and the pair stands there for the rest of the run. Measured at 15°: six stalls of 8 s or more over ten seeds, two of them over 800 s of a 960 s run. At 20° and above they do not occur. That is why this slider's low end is interesting to watch and not worth using.
The other direction is a real trade. The 20° / 70 px default roughly doubles throughput against 45° / 90 px, because a robot that never sees the other one coming never stops for it — the traffic it avoids avoiding is avoidance, not collisions, and physical overlap stays at zero either way. Every other number recorded in warehouse_sim.js was measured at 45° / 90 px, so drag both back there to reproduce them.
Range and angle are separate knobs on purpose: a narrow cone at long range still sees an approaching robot in time, whereas a narrow cone at 70 px sees the same robot well under half a second before contact.

The angle affects robot-vs-robot detection only: a rack is found by a straight ray down the heading, deliberately not by the cone, so that a robot aimed through a 50 px aisle does not read the flanking rack corners as a wall ahead (warehouse_sim.js, rayHitsRack).

Router switches the path planner. grid plans over a uniform 50 px lattice with Dijkstra, so its routes are 17% shorter and none of its legs crosses a rack. chain is the original hand-built corridor-and-aisle router and keeps every robot on three lanes; on three lanes a meeting is reliably head-on and the priority election fires, whereas the grid's ~20 lanes leave robots passing offset and both dodging, which is why the chain arm delivered 36% more (1029 against 662) at the old defaults. Under the new short-narrow-lidar defaults the grid arm wins instead (1555 against 1303), so the pair is worth comparing live rather than taken on trust.
